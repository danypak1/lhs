# Chapter 1 — The Scientific Study of Life
## High-Yield Summary — everything examinable on one page

---

### 1. What science is

**Science** = an intellectual activity involving **observation, description, experimentation & explanation of the natural world** (the objective, measurable world).

- Science is **something you do**, not a collection of facts
- Science **cannot** answer questions about immeasurable things: religion, morals, ethics, values, art
- A scientist is a detective for the natural world: logic, evidence, objectivity — never hunches

### 2. The scientific method — know the order

```
observation → question → hypothesis → prediction → experiment → conclusion
                (rejected? → new hypothesis, loop again)
```

| Term | Is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Tentative **explanation**, must be **testable** | "Car won't start because the battery is dead" |
| **Prediction** | **If/then** consequence of the hypothesis | "*If* so, *then* it starts with a new battery" |

Toaster example: outlet-fault hypothesis, coffeemaker works in that outlet → hypothesis **rejected** → form a new one. A rejected hypothesis is progress, not failure.

### 3. The two principles of experimental setup ⭐ (10-point graded question, W1)

1. **Test only ONE variable at a time** — more than one at once = **confounds**
2. **Use controls** to keep all other variables the same between groups

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Variable | Changeable characteristic of an experiment |
| Experimental group | Gets the treatment (the tested variable) |
| Control group | Identical **except** the tested variable |
| **Placebo** | Fake pill, **no active ingredient** — controls for belief (given to the control group) |
| Blind / double-blind | Subjects / subjects **and** assessors don't know group assignments |
| Randomisation | Chance assignment → equivalent groups |
| Sample size | Bigger = less vulnerable to chance (echinacea trial: 437 volunteers, randomised, echinacea vs placebo, same virus → **no effect**) |

Andersson's widowbirds: cut-and-reglued-tail controls separate tail *length* from *handling* → females prefer long tails.

### 4. Trust filters

- **Repeatability:** once is never enough; big follow-ups overturn small studies (2005 "epilepsy drug cures AIDS" — killed by huge 2008 study). Conflicting studies → **go with the majority**, ignore lone outliers
- **Peer review:** other scientists vet methods, data, conclusions before publication; not all journals do it anymore; names hidden to fight (subconscious) **bias**
- **Pseudoscience** = claims to be factual but is **incompatible with the scientific method** (unfalsifiable)
- **Cognitive bias** = mental shortcut → selective, subjective processing → irrational judgments
- **People who want your money:** Airborne's "clinical study" **never existed**; **Wakefield** faked MMR-autism data (patented rival vaccine, paid via a solicitor acting for suing parents) → retracted, licence lost — but vaccine fear persists and eradicated diseases returned
- **Sensationalism:** headlines exaggerate; check sample size, controls, peer review, repetition

### 5. Hypothesis vs. theory · basic vs. applied

- **Scientific theory** = broad explanation of many phenomena backed by a **large body of repeated evidence** (cell theory, evolution, germ theory). The strongest statement in science — "just a theory" is a language trap
- **Basic science** = knowledge for its own sake · **Applied science** = solving a specific practical problem. Applied is built on basic (bacterial immunity → CRISPR)

### 6. Characteristics of life — need ALL six

| # | Characteristic | Trap to remember |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Organised** | atoms → molecules → cells |
| 2 | **Requires energy & nutrients** | nutrients **cycle**, energy **flows through** (in via photosynthesis, out as heat) |
| 3 | **Maintains homeostasis** | internal constancy despite external change (temp, BP, pH); "life = negentropy" |
| 4 | **Grows & develops** | DNA-guided; you grew from one cell |
| 5 | **Reproduces** | **species level**, not individual — sterile mule is alive; must use the organism's **own** machinery, which is what excludes viruses |
| 6 | **Evolves** | genetic change in a **population** over generations — **individuals never evolve** |

No simple definition of life — parts in a test tube ≠ alive. Fire/crystals have *some* characteristics → not alive. **The cell is the smallest unit of life.**

### 7. Levels of organisation

```
atom → molecule → organelle → CELL → tissue → organ → organ system
→ organism → population → community → ecosystem → biosphere
```

**Population** = one species in an area · **Community** = all species · **Ecosystem** = community **+ nonliving environment**.

### 8. Taxonomy & domains

Hierarchy: **Domain → Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species** (Dear King Philip Came Over For Good Soup).

| Domain | Cells |
|---|---|
| **Bacteria** | Prokaryotic (no nucleus) |
| **Archaea** | Prokaryotic; often extreme environments |
| **Eukarya** | Eukaryotic (nucleus): protists, fungi, plants, animals |

Binomial name = *Genus species*, italicised, genus capitalised: ***Homo sapiens***.

## The 8 sentences most likely to appear on the exam

1. Science studies the measurable natural world and cannot answer questions of morals, values, religion or art.
2. A hypothesis is a testable explanation; a prediction is its if/then consequence.
3. The two principles of experimental setup: test only one variable at a time, and use controls to keep all other variables the same.
4. A placebo is a fake pill with no active ingredient, given to the control group to eliminate belief as a confound.
5. Experiments must be repeated to be trusted; when studies conflict, go with the majority.
6. A scientific theory is a broad, heavily evidenced explanation — far stronger than a hypothesis.
7. Living things share all six characteristics: organisation, energy & nutrients, homeostasis, growth & development, reproduction (species level) and evolution (population level).
8. The cell is the smallest unit of life, and all life falls into three domains: Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya.
